## 1. Sequence retrieval and genome-level curation

Complete genome records for herpes simplex virus 1 (NCBI taxid 10298) and herpes simplex
virus 2 (taxid 10310) were retrieved from NCBI Virus using the NCBI `datasets` CLI
(bundled in `bin/`). Retrieval returned 1,305 records.

Records were excluded at the genome level if they were shorter than the type-specific
length window, carried more than 1% ambiguous bases, were annotated as laboratory-adapted,
engineered, recombinant or synthetic constructs, or duplicated another record's accession
content. Of 1,305 records, 417 passed outright (`PASS`), 29 were flagged but kept
(`FLAGGED`) and 859 were excluded; every decision is listed with its reason in
`19_supplements/S3_removed_sequences.tsv`. Of the 859 excluded, 229 HSV-2 records were
subsequently readmitted as a tier-2 cohort (next paragraph but one), so the number of
records excluded from all analysis is 630, not 888.

Virus type was verified computationally rather than taken from the database label. Each
genome was compared against both type references (HSV-1 NC_001806.2, HSV-2 NC_001798.2)
and assigned by average nucleotide identity. 80 records showed a disagreement between the
database label and the computed assignment and were handled as recorded in
`07_qc_reports/04e_species_verification.tsv`; the computed assignment was used throughout.

Genomes entering per-gene extraction: 649 in total — 303 HSV-1, 117 HSV-2 tier 1, and
229 HSV-2 tier 2. (These file counts exceed the 417 `PASS` records above because they
include the flagged-but-kept records, one reference anchor per type, and the tier-2 set
drawn from the excluded pool; `02_filtered_genomes/`, not the filtering log, defines what
entered extraction.)

**The tier-2 set is not analysed separately, and this materially qualifies every HSV-2
result.** HSV-2 is far more sparsely sequenced than HSV-1, and applying the ≤1% ambiguity
rule strictly leaves cohorts too small for selection inference. A tier-2 set of 229 HSV-2
genomes — excluded at tier 1, overwhelmingly for ambiguity between 1% and 2% N — was
therefore readmitted and pooled with tier 1 for all downstream work. In the final analysis
cohorts, **178 of the 286 HSV-2 accessions (62%) are tier-2 genomes**; the HSV-1 cohorts
contain none. Per-sequence tier is recorded in the `cohort_tier=` field of every FASTA
header and in `07_qc_reports/04f_tier2_qa_report.tsv`. The CDS-level QC of §2 was applied
to tier-2 sequences on the same terms as tier 1, so the readmitted genomes still had to
yield intact reading frames; what is relaxed is the genome-level ambiguity threshold, and
the consequence is that HSV-2 inference rests on assemblies of lower average quality than
HSV-1 inference. This is a limitation, not a correction, and is reported as such.

## 2. Per-gene CDS extraction and quality control

Twenty genes were selected a priori as the analysis set: the entry and surface
glycoproteins (US6/gD, UL27/gB, UL22/gH, UL1/gL, US8/gE, US7/gI, US4/gG, UL53/gK,
UL10/gM, UL49A/gN), the immediate-early and regulatory proteins (RL2/ICP0, RS1/ICP4,
UL54/ICP27), the tegument proteins (UL48/VP16, UL46, UL47, UL49/VP22), and the enzymes
(UL23/TK, UL44/gC, UL30/DNA polymerase).

Coding sequences were extracted by reference-guided annotation transfer and assessed
independently of extraction success. A CDS passed QC only if it began with ATG, ended in a
stop codon, had a length divisible by three, contained no internal stop codon, and carried
ambiguous bases below the per-gene threshold. A length-ratio window of 0.95–1.05 against
the reference CDS was also applied.

**12,054 QC-passed CDS were carried forward** (`05_cds_qc_passed/`), and these are what
deduplication and every later stage operate on. The per-sequence QC log
`07_qc_reports/07_cds_qc_log.tsv` records 11,504 assessments, of which 11,340 passed and
164 failed, and so does not cover the full carried-forward set: 697 sequences present in
the QC-passed FASTAs have no row in it, because they were added by a later extraction
top-up that updated `07_qc_reports/06_cds_extraction_log.tsv` and the FASTAs but not the
QC log. All 686 of those that appear in the extraction log have `extraction_status=SUCCESS`,
an ATG start, a valid stop codon, `in_frame=True` and a length 92.9–102.4% of reference, so
they satisfy every criterion above except that the length-ratio window would exclude a few
of them. The figures to cite are therefore 12,054 carried forward and 4,163 unique after
deduplication; 11,340/164 describes the QC log's coverage, not the dataset.

### 2.2 A QC criterion was missing for RL2 and RS1

`step07_gaps_abc.py` rebuilds the RL2 and RS1 QC-passed FASTAs, because those two
repeat-resident genes needed the §2.1 treatment. Its own comment claimed it applied
"identical Step 7 criteria", but it checked only start codon, stop codon, frame and
internal stops: the **ambiguity** test (`%N < 0.5`) and the length-ratio window were
absent. RL2 and RS1 therefore entered the analysis having passed a weaker test than the
other eighteen genes, and **81 sequences carrying 0.5–16.8% ambiguous bases (median 2.2%)
reached the final cohorts** — 46 in HSV-1 RS1, 21 in HSV-2 RS1, 12 in HSV-2 RL2, 2 in
HSV-1 RL2. The script has been corrected; the cohort analysed here has not been re-filtered,
for the reason below.

The measured effect on the analysis is small, because occupancy masking (§5) removes the
repeat regions where the ambiguity is concentrated — RS1/HSV-1 loses 63.4% of its codons and
RL2/HSV-2 37.0%. In the alignments actually analysed, ambiguous bases are **0.007–0.042% of
all cells**, and the worst single column has an `N` in 7.0% of sequences (HSV-2 RS1);
HyPhy treats `N` as missing data, which costs information rather than creating false
substitutions. Re-filtering would shrink the two smallest HSV-2 cohorts further
(RL2 n=75, RS1 n=57) and would invalidate every alignment, tree, GARD partition and HyPhy
fit for these genes, including a GARD search that took over sixteen hours. The cohort is
therefore pinned by accession in `19_supplements/S2_final_accessions.tsv` and the defect is
reported with its measured impact. Where it does matter is at the level of an individual
site, and §8.1 shows exactly that: one of the variants behind the RL2/HSV-1 residue 308
call comes from a sequence that fails the ambiguity test.

A further 19 sequences sit in the QC-passed FASTAs despite a `FAIL` status, of which **8
reach the final analysis** — all HSV-1 US7/gI, all failing only the length-ratio window at
ratios 1.054–1.087. `07_qc_reports/07c_length_filter_audit.tsv` shows US7 is the one gene
with a systematic upper-bound excess (19 of 292 HSV-1 sequences), and
`07b_us7_failure_breakdown.tsv` shows the excess is a clean in-frame +84 nt (+28 codon)
insertion with no internal stop and 0% ambiguity. These are real gI length polymorphisms
rather than assembly errors, so they were retained deliberately; the length-ratio window,
unlike the other criteria, is a heuristic for mis-annotation and not a correctness test.

### 2.1 Repeat-resident genes (RL2, RS1)

RL2 (ICP0) and RS1 (ICP4) lie in the genome repeat regions and showed elevated QC failure
rates. Nucleotide-level alignment of the failing sequences classified 70–100% of those
failures as real clinical indels and tandem-repeat length variation in GC-rich domains
rather than assembly error, so these genes were retained with their reduced cohorts
(RL2: 187 HSV-1 / 75 HSV-2 unique sequences; RS1: 215 / 57 after deduplication). The
reduced HSV-2 RS1 count reflects documented assembly variability across the ICP4 repeat
and is reported as a limitation, not corrected for.

## 3. Deduplication with isolate weighting

Exactly identical coding sequences within a gene × type cohort were collapsed to one
representative, reducing the 12,054 QC-passed CDS of §2 to 4,163 unique sequences. The number of
input isolates each representative stands for (`cluster_size`) was retained and carried
forward, so that every population-frequency quantity downstream — per-residue identity,
Shannon entropy, allele frequencies — is computed over the 12,054 isolates represented
rather than over the 4,163 unique haplotypes. Both weightings are reported in
`11_conservation/per_residue_conservation.tsv`; the isolate-weighted values are the ones
used for inference.

Deduplication is not an attempt at patient-level independence; see §9.

## 4. Codon-aware multiple sequence alignment

Protein sequences were aligned with FAMSA (Deorowicz, Debudaj-Grabysz & Gudyś,
*Sci Rep* 6:33964, 2016; via `pyfamsa` 0.7.0, single-linkage guide tree), and the
nucleotide alignment was produced by back-projecting each codon onto its aligned amino
acid. This is the MACSE strategy — align proteins, project codons — and guarantees that
every alignment column is a codon column in a single consistent frame. FAMSA was used in
place of MAFFT/MACSE because it is pip-installable, deterministic, and benchmarks at or
above MAFFT's accuracy on protein families of this size; no manual refinement was applied.

The type reference protein was included as an anchor row (`REF_<accession>|<gene>`) so
that every alignment column has a defined reference-residue coordinate. The anchor is
removed before any analysis that treats rows as observations (trees, selection); it is
excluded from occupancy and conservation counts throughout.

Each alignment was validated against hard invariants before being written: length
divisible by three, no internal stop codon in any row, every row's ungapped nucleotide
sequence identical to its input, and the back-projection consistent with the protein
alignment. All 40 per-type alignments pass (`06_alignments_v2/alignment_qc_v2.tsv`);
mean pairwise nucleotide identity 99.31%, mean gap content 4.46%.

Combined HSV-1 + HSV-2 alignments were also built, and are used **only** for
between-type conservation comparison. They are not used for phylogenetics, recombination
detection or selection inference, because a pooled alignment of two virus species is a
between-species comparison: amino-acid identity between types ranges from 37.5% (US4/gG)
to 90.8% across the gene set, and any method that assumes a single interbreeding
population will recover the species split rather than within-population structure. The
files carry a `CONSERVATION USE ONLY` marker.

## 5. Occupancy masking and coordinate mapping

Alignment columns were dropped where fewer than 50% of **clinical** sequences (reference
anchor excluded) carried a residue. Masking operates on whole codons so the reading frame
is preserved. Across the 40 per-type cohorts, 4,037 codon columns were dropped; the median
cohort lost 0% of its columns and the maximum loss was 63.4%, concentrated in the
repeat-resident genes where the length variation of §2.1 is real.

Masking changed mean within-cohort identity from 99.31% to 99.32%, i.e. it removes
low-occupancy columns without materially inflating the conservation estimate — the
alignment was already in-frame and in-register, so masking is a tidying step rather than a
correction.

For every cohort, a coordinate map (`06_alignments_v2/coordinate_maps/`) records, per
column, the unmasked alignment column, the masked codon column, and the corresponding
reference residue number. All downstream residue-level claims — selected sites, epitope
ranges, structural positions — are reported in reference-residue numbering via this map,
so that positions are comparable across cohorts and to the literature.

Sequences whose distance from the cohort consensus exceeded 6 median absolute deviations
were flagged (163 across all per-type cohorts) and **retained**, not removed; they are
listed in `06_alignments_v2/outlier_sequences_v2.tsv` for inspection. Removing divergent
sequences before a selection analysis would remove the signal being measured.

## 6. Phylogenetic inference

Maximum-likelihood trees were inferred per gene × type with IQ-TREE 2.4.0, substitution
model chosen by ModelFinder (`-m MFP`), 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates, and
identical sequences kept (`--keep-ident`) so that branch support reflects the cohort as
analysed. Sequence identifiers were replaced with short safe IDs (`S0001`-style) for tool
compatibility; `06_alignments_v2/hyphy_ready/<type>/<gene>_idmap.tsv` maps each back to its
accession and `cluster_size`.

Four cohorts (UL27 and UL47 in HSV-1, UL30 and UL46 in HSV-2) aborted the default
likelihood kernel with `Numerical underflow (lh-derivative)`, a consequence of the very
short branches in these low-divergence within-type cohorts. They were re-inferred with
IQ-TREE's higher-precision `-safe` kernel; the `safe_kernel` column of
`06_alignments_v2/trees_v2/tree_summary_v2.tsv` records which trees this applies to. All
40 trees carry a completed `.iqtree` report, i.e. bootstrapping finished rather than the
run having been interrupted after the point estimate was written.

No combined-cohort trees were built, for the reason given in §4.

## 7. Recombination screening

GARD (Kosakovsky Pond et al.) was run per gene × type, on the same alignments used for
selection, and **before** the selection analysis so that any detected breakpoint
partitions the alignment that selection is then run on. HyPhy 2.5.103(MP) was executed in
the pinned `hyphy/hyphy` Docker image with `TOLERATE_NUMERICAL_ERRORS=1`, which HyPhy
recommends for the very low-divergence alignments these within-type cohorts produce;
exact version, image digest and CPU count are recorded in
`14_selection/gard_provenance.json`.

Where GARD reports a breakpoint, the alignment is split at codon boundaries (breakpoints
snapped outward to the enclosing codon) and a partition-specific tree is inferred for each
segment before selection is run on it. The 37 partition trees were built under a fixed
GTR+F+G4 model **without** ModelFinder and **without** bootstrap replicates, unlike the 40
whole-cohort trees of §6: they serve only as fixed topologies for the HyPhy fits, which do
not read branch support, and the short partitions give ModelFinder little to discriminate.
Partition spans were verified to tile each alignment exactly, on codon boundaries, with the
same sequence set as the parent cohort, and each partition tree is bound to its alignment
by a stored content hash (`.alnmd5`) so that a changed breakpoint cannot silently reuse an
old topology.

All 40 gene × type cohorts completed. **Sixteen** carry at least one breakpoint and
**24** none, giving **37** partitions from the partitioned cohorts and **61** analysis
units in total (37 partitions + 24 whole alignments). Per-cohort breakpoint positions,
ΔAICc values, model counts and exported partition ranges are in
`14_selection/gard_summary_per_type.tsv`.

- **UL44 / HSV-1** is retained at ΔAICc 3.70, which is marginal on GARD's

was the HSV-1/HSV-2 species split being read as recombination, not recombination within
either type. It is one of the concrete reasons the pooled-cohort results were withdrawn
(`10_hyphy_outputs/DEPRECATED_DO_NOT_USE.md`).

## 8. Selection inference and multiple-testing correction

For each gene × type cohort, and separately for each GARD partition
where one exists, five HyPhy methods are run in a single pass, with no gating of one
method on another's result: BUSTED (gene-wide episodic diversifying selection), FEL
(pervasive site-level selection), MEME (episodic site-level selection), FUBAR (Bayesian
site-level selection) and aBSREL (branch-level selection).

Significance policy, fixed in advance:

- FEL and MEME site p-values are corrected by Benjamini–Hochberg within each
  gene × cohort × partition × method family; sites are reported at q < 0.05.
- BUSTED gene-level p-values are corrected by Benjamini–Hochberg across the whole gene
  family tested.
- aBSREL branch results use the method's own Holm–Bonferroni corrected p-values.
- FUBAR sites are reported at posterior probability ≥ 0.95, with ≥ 0.90 as a secondary,
  explicitly labelled tier.
- A site is called under selection in the primary analysis only if at least two methods
  agree, with the direction of selection taken from β versus α.

Every reported site is mapped through the §5 coordinate map from its partition offset to
masked codon column to reference residue, so sites from partitioned and unpartitioned
cohorts are reported in one coordinate system. Final tables:
`14_selection/site_level_results.tsv`, `gene_level_results.tsv`,
`branch_level_results.tsv`, `selection_concordance.tsv`, `selected_sites_final.tsv`.

### Results

The screen comprises **305 HyPhy runs** (61 analysis units × 5 methods), all of which
completed and carry a completion marker; no result in these tables comes from an
interrupted run.

### 8.1 Variant support, and why the two concordant sites are not reported as findings

A significance threshold says nothing about how many sequences carry the variant that
produced the signal. On alignments this conserved that question is decisive: a single
non-synonymous singleton at an otherwise invariant codon drives β/α towards infinity, and
MEME will fire with an essentially unbounded β. `step26_site_robustness.py` therefore
annotates every reported site with its variant support, and the rule was fixed before
looking at the outcome — a site is `NOT_ROBUST` if fewer than 5 of the thousands of
isolates represented carry the variant, or if every supporting sequence fails CDS QC.

**Both sites that satisfy the ≥ 2-method concordance rule are `NOT_ROBUST`:**

| | RL2/ICP0, HSV-1, residue 308 | US7/gI, HSV-1, residue 155 |
|---|---|---|
| Majority codon | GGC (Gly) × 183 | CCA (Pro) × 125 |
| Variants | CGC × 1, TCC × 2 | GAC × 1, TCA × 1 |
| Isolates carrying a variant | 4 of 293 (1.4%) | 2 of 276 (0.7%) |
| Largest codon change | 2 nt | 3 nt |
| FUBAR posterior | 0.9560 | 0.9523 |
| MEME q | 0.0246 (β̂ = 1,024) | 0.0051 (β̂ = 1,842) |
| FEL | p = 0.169, not significant | q = 0.637, not significant |
| Supporting accessions | HM585511, OP431901, OR771687 — **OR771687 fails the §2.2 ambiguity test** | PP379031, PP379033 — **adjacent accessions, i.e. one submission batch** |

Every indicator points the same way. The support is two to four isolates; the FUBAR
posteriors sit a thousandth above the 0.95 cut; the MEME β estimates are the unbounded
values characteristic of a near-invariant column; FEL supports neither; a 3-nucleotide
codon change observed once is far more consistent with a sequencing or assembly artifact
than with a point substitution; one site's only two variants come from the same submission
batch and the other's includes a sequence that should not have passed QC.

**These two sites are therefore reported as candidate artifacts, not as evidence of
positive selection.** `selected_sites_final.tsv` is left unchanged so that the
pre-registered rule's output stands on the record, and `site_robustness.tsv` qualifies it.

The wider picture is the reverse of what the concordance rule suggests. Of the 92 sites
called by at least one method, **76 are `ROBUST`** (median 44 of ~300 isolates carrying the
variant), 7 are `WEAK` and 9 are `NOT_ROBUST`. The robust ones are almost all FUBAR-only,
so they are well supported in the data but rest on a single method. **No site in this study
is both multi-method concordant and robustly supported**, and that, not the two-site table,
is the honest summary of the site-level screen. It is also the expected outcome for genes
with 99.4% (HSV-1) and 99.7% (HSV-2) mean within-type identity, and it is consistent with
the gene-level and branch-level results, which do not depend on single columns.

The lesson for the method is specific: requiring two methods to agree does not make a site
more reliable when one of the methods is prone to firing on singletons. Concordance and
variant support are independent checks and both are needed.

The gap between 92 sites at ≥ 1 method and 2 at ≥ 2 methods is the expected behaviour of
the concordance rule, not an anomaly: FEL, MEME and FUBAR differ in what they test
(pervasive vs episodic vs Bayesian posterior) and the within-type cohorts carry little
divergence, so single-method hits are common and agreement is rare. Both tiers are
reported — `selection_concordance.tsv` for the 92, `selected_sites_final.tsv` for the 2 —
and only the 2-method set feeds the §14 exclusion filter.

The effect of the correction is substantial and worth stating plainly. Within single
cohorts, FEL returned 35 sites at raw p < 0.05 for US6/HSV-1 of which 2 survive at
q < 0.05, and 33 for UL54/HSV-1 of which none do. Applying this same policy
retrospectively to the earlier uncorrected results reduced 5,126 sites at raw p < 0.10 to
1,332 at q < 0.05, and 19 of the 46 analysis units in that earlier run were pooled
two-species cohorts that are excluded here.

## 9. Sampling bias, patient structure, and what cannot be tested

Patient identity is not recorded in these records. A patient identifier was derived
conservatively: from serial-isolate naming where present (`<patient>_d<day>_...`), else
BioSample, else isolate code, else the accession as a singleton
(`03_metadata/patient_assignments.tsv`). One-per-patient cohorts were constructed so that
any analysis sensitive to pseudo-replication can be repeated on them.

Two comparisons in the original design are **not feasible** on this data and are withdrawn
rather than reported weakly:

- **Anatomical site / CNS versus mucosal.** `isolation_source` is populated for 0.08% of
  records and exactly one record carries a CNS or encephalitis indication.
- **Geographic and temporal stratification.** Country is unknown for 82.45% of records
  (1,076 of 1,305, spanning 1,024 derived patients) and collection year for 91.6%. The
  remaining records are dominated by a single country (USA, 13.0%).

`03_metadata/metadata_feasibility_statement.md` records these determinations with the
counts, and `03_metadata/sampling_bias_tables.tsv` gives the full distributions. All
results are therefore reported as a pooled global analysis with an explicit statement that
the cohort is a convenience sample with unknown geographic composition.

## 10. Conservation

Per-residue Shannon entropy and percent identity were computed within each type and
between types, isolate-weighted by `cluster_size` (§3). Gene-level means are averaged only
over columns with ≥ 50% occupancy **for the type in question**, with occupancy counts
reported alongside: gap-only columns otherwise enter a type's mean as 0% identity, which
for genes whose two types differ greatly in length (US4/gG is 238 aa in HSV-1 and 699 aa in
HSV-2) produces a meaningless figure.

Mean within-type identity across the 20 genes is 99.38% (HSV-1) and 99.73% (HSV-2); mean
between-type identity ranges from 37.5% to 90.8%. 283 conserved windows were identified
(`11_conservation/conserved_windows.tsv`). The high within-type identity is itself the
central constraint on this study's power: these are strongly conserved coding sequences,
and a selection analysis on them has limited power to detect site-level diversifying
selection. That is a property of the virus, and is stated as such rather than compensated
for by relaxing thresholds.

## 11. Experimentally validated epitopes

Epitopes were retrieved from the IEDB Query API for both taxids and curated to human-host
records with a positive assay outcome whose source antigen matched one of the 20 reference
proteins exactly. 688 records were retained and 4,118 excluded
(`12_iedb/iedb_curated_epitopes.tsv`, `iedb_excluded_epitopes.tsv`).

Excluded records are **not** negative labels, and no negative epitope set is constructed
anywhere in this work. Absence of an IEDB record means the peptide has not been tested, not
that it is non-immunogenic. This is why the §12 benchmark is described as measuring
recovery of known epitopes rather than discrimination.

## 12. HLA class I prediction and its validation

NetMHCpan-4.1 requires an academic licence that cannot be obtained non-interactively, so
MHCflurry 2.3.30 (`Class1PresentationPredictor`; O'Donnell et al., *Cell Syst* 11:42, 2020)
was used as the free, already-trained equivalent. **Nothing is trained or tuned in this
work**; the predictor is used as an instrument, with its published models and thresholds.

All 9- and 10-mers were enumerated from the type reference proteins, so every prediction
carries a reference-residue range, and each peptide was annotated with the §10
conservation values. Predictions were made for each of the 27 alleles of the Weiskopf/Sette
class I reference panel separately (not best-allele-only), giving a peptide × allele table
required by §13. Binder definition: predicted affinity ≤ 500 nM or presentation score
≥ 0.50.

Validation (`15_benchmark/`) is gene-grouped: for each gene × type the enumerated peptides
are scored against the §11 curated epitopes, so that performance is not inflated by one
well-studied antigen. IEDB records of 8–14 aa were used as positives; longer records are
overlapping peptide-scan constructs rather than single class I ligands, and including them
labels nearly every enumerated 9-mer as positive. Over 27 scorable gene × type groups
(17 HSV-1 and 10 HSV-2, spanning 17 distinct genes; the `genes_scored` field of
`benchmark_summary.tsv` counts the distinct genes, not the groups), predicted affinity gives
mean AUC 0.809 (median 0.892, range 0.425–0.997), mean average precision 0.326 and 6.0-fold
enrichment of known epitopes in the top decile over 428 positives. The split by type is
stark: HSV-1 groups give mean AUC 0.931, HSV-2 groups 0.600. Conservation alone performs near chance (mean AUC 0.565,
1.35-fold top-decile enrichment), which is the result that justifies using conservation and
predicted binding as two independent filters rather than either on its own.

Low-AUC groups are concentrated in HSV-2, where the IEDB records are fewer and are largely
inherited from HSV-1 cross-reactivity studies; these are reported, not excluded.

## 13. Population coverage

Coverage was computed from the IEDB population coverage allele-frequency data (tool version
and SHA-256 recorded in `18_population_coverage/allele_frequency_provenance.json`) using the
published method (Bui et al., *BMC Bioinformatics* 7:153, 2006): per locus,
coverage = 1 − (1 − Σf)², and combined coverage = 1 − Π(1 − coverage_locus). The formula was
reimplemented rather than the downloaded tool executed; the downloaded data file was read
with a restricted unpickler that admits only `collections.OrderedDict`, so no code from the
download can execute.

Across 239 class I populations, the cross-protective shortlist covers 99.71% of the World
population, with a median of 97.5% across populations and 73.6% of populations above 90%.
A greedy minimal panel reaches 90.6% World coverage with one peptide and 99.2% with two.

Three caveats are reported with these figures. (i) HLA-C is absent from the 27-allele
panel, so combined class I coverage is conservative. (ii) Coverage saturates: because the
metric counts alleles with at least one binder, the three shortlist tiers give identical
coverage once every panel allele is hit, so coverage discriminates panels, not shortlist
depth — the per-population average hit counts in `coverage_by_population.tsv` are the
quantity that distinguishes them. (iii) Populations with sparse frequency records in the
IEDB dataset return low coverage for that reason and not biologically (Central America,
7.76%, has very few typed alleles in the dataset); these are reported unadjusted and
flagged.

### 13.1 Class II coverage

Class II coverage is computed from the same file and the same formula, but it needs two
departures that are properties of the data rather than choices, and both change the answer
materially.

**DP and DQ molecule coverage is bracketed, not computed.** The IEDB file stores class II
frequencies per *chain* — HLA-DRB1, HLA-DPA1, HLA-DPB1, HLA-DQA1, HLA-DQB1 — while a DP or
DQ molecule is a heterodimer and NetMHCIIpan predicts for the αβ pair. There is no pair
frequency in the file, so the pair has to be derived from its chains, and the two obvious
derivations bound the truth from opposite sides. The **upper bound** treats each chain
locus as contributing independently, exactly as the class I calculation does; it
overestimates, because carrying a covered DQA1 chain does not mean the individual forms a
covered DQ molecule. The **conservative** figure requires both chains,
cov(DP) = cov(DPA1) × cov(DPB1); it underestimates, because DQA1 and DQB1 are in strong
linkage disequilibrium and real haplotypes pair them far more often than independence
implies. Both are reported throughout, and neither is presented as *the* class II coverage.

**Only populations typed at all five presenting loci are summarised.** Populations in this
file differ in which class II loci were typed: of 274, only 64 have all five, while 28 have
just one. A population with no HLA-DRB1 record is not a population with zero DR coverage —
it is one that cannot be scored at DR. Scoring the missing loci as absent coverage produces
false zeros, and the effect is not small: it puts the mean conservative coverage at 82.3%
instead of 98.9%, and the resulting pattern looks like a geographic finding rather than a
typing artifact. Every row in `coverage_by_population_classii.tsv` therefore carries
`loci_typed` and `scorable`, and the summary statistics use the scorable subset. This
differs from the class I treatment in §13 above, which reports sparse populations
unadjusted and flagged; the class I panel spans two loci, where the failure mode is a low
value, whereas missing a class II locus zeroes a whole term.

**HLA-DRB3, -DRB4 and -DRB5 have no frequency data in this file at all**, so 4 of the 27
panel alleles cannot contribute and the DR term is DRB1-only. Both figures are conservative
in that respect, and it is why these numbers do not reproduce IEDB's published >99%
coverage for this panel.

With those three stated, across the 64 fully typed populations the 27-allele panel gives
mean conservative coverage **98.87%** (median 99.63%, minimum 90.75% in South Korea; upper
bound 99.98%), and World coverage of **99.64% to 100%**. Combining with class I over the 51
populations present in both frequency sets gives mean **99.98%** and World **100%**.

As with class I, these figures describe the **allele panel**, not the shortlist: with 22,624
peptides every panel allele has at least one predicted binder, so the weak-or-better and
strong-binder sets return identical coverage and the number is at the panel's ceiling. It
is not evidence that any particular peptide set is broadly presented.

## 14. Candidate integration and ranking

Candidates are assembled by joining predicted binding (§12), per-residue conservation
(§10), known-epitope flags (§11), N-linked glycosylation sequons (`N[^P][ST]`, which can
occlude a B-cell epitope), and the positive-selection filter (§8). The priority score is a
fixed, pre-declared linear combination of percentile ranks —
0.45 × binding + 0.35 × conservation + 0.15 × conserved-in-both-types
− 0.05 × glycosylation-sequon overlap — and is a transparent ordering device, not a
trained model or a predicted probability of immunogenicity.

Two quality flags are carried on every candidate and should travel with any use of the
shortlist. First, `low_complexity` marks peptides drawn from the serine-, glycine-,
alanine- and proline-rich tandem repeats of ICP0 and ICP4 — peptides such as `SSSSSSSSA`
and `GAGGGGGGR`, which are perfectly conserved and score as binders but are not credible
epitopes, and which cannot even be assigned a unique position (`SAASSSSSA` occurs at
HSV-2 RL2 595, 602 and 609). **1,527 of the 9,450 primary rows (16.2%) are low-complexity**,
and 386 of the 3,202 cross-protective rows; only 1 appears in the top 50 by priority score,
so the ranking already suppresses them, but they are flagged rather than dropped so that the
shortlist size is not changed silently by a filter added after the fact. Second, the
positive-selection exclusion removes only 9 rows, and §8.1 explains that the two sites
driving it are themselves probable artifacts — so this filter is close to a no-op here and
should not be presented as having shaped the shortlist.

The selection filter is applied for real: `selection_filter_applied` is `True` for every
row of `16_shortlist/epitope_candidates_ranked.tsv` (the script writes
`NOT_YET_APPLIED` into that column instead, and labels the shortlist provisional, if it
is run before §8 has results). Of 12,771 ranked candidates, **9** overlap one of the two
positively selected residues (5 peptides spanning US7 155, 4 spanning RL2 308) and are
excluded from the primary shortlist.

Final shortlist: **9,450 rows** (predicted binder, every residue ≥ 99.0% conserved within
type, not positively selected), of which **3,202 rows** are identical across both types and
form the cross-protective subset (`16_shortlist/shortlist_primary.tsv`,
`shortlist_cross_protective.tsv`). A row is one peptide in one virus type, so a peptide
conserved across both types appears twice; in distinct peptide sequences the shortlist is
**8,070** peptides and the cross-protective subset **2,120**. The distinct-peptide counts
are the ones to quote as "candidate epitopes", and they are what §13 computes coverage over.

The top-ranked candidates are dominated by HLA-A*02:03 and the A2 supertype, which is a
known property of class I predictors on hydrophobic 9-mers and not a biological finding;
§13's per-allele coverage analysis is what establishes that the shortlist as a whole is
not A2-restricted.

As a positive control, the cross-protective shortlist recovers `RMLGDVMAV` — a published
HLA-A*02 gB epitope — at **rank 11 of 3,202** by priority score, correctly flagged as
IEDB-known. Its coordinates are gB 559–567 in HSV-2 numbering and 562–570 in HSV-1, and it
is the HSV-2 row that enters the shortlist.

## 15. Structural mapping

Candidates in the three structurally characterised entry glycoproteins were mapped onto
crystal structures: 2C36 (gD), 2GUM (gB) and 3M1C (gH–gL). Chains were assigned to genes by
BLOSUM62 alignment of the structure's observed sequence against the reference protein,
requiring ≥ 80% identity; 3,145 residues across 7 chains were assigned at 0.995–1.000
identity. Relative solvent accessibility was computed with Biopython's Shrake–Rupley
implementation normalised by the Tien et al. (2013) maximum ASA values, and residues with
RSA ≥ 0.25 were called exposed.

Conserved, exposed surface patches were required to be contiguous in **both** the structure
and the reference numbering, so that a patch cannot span an unresolved loop. 33 such
patches were identified; `17_structures/conserved_exposed_patches.tsv` lists them per chain
(multimeric structures contribute one row per chain, so the same reference range appears
once per copy). PyMOL session scripts are written to `17_structures/figures/`.

Notable: gB 257–264 (`SRVEAFHR`) is fully conserved within both types, exposed, and
known-epitope-annotated at all eight residues.

## 16. B-cell epitope prediction

An earlier draft of these methods listed the whole B-cell arm as blocked on DTU HealthTech
academic registration, alongside NetMHCIIpan-4.3. That was wrong for two of the three
tools. Only NetMHCIIpan is distributed exclusively through the registration form;
BepiPred-3.0 and DiscoTope-3.0 are published as source under licences that permit academic
non-commercial use, so both were installed and run locally. The correction is recorded
rather than quietly absorbed, because the earlier statement "no B-cell claim is made"
appeared in the results draft and in the README and had to be withdrawn.

Both predictors are run on the **type reference protein** (and, for DiscoTope-3.0, on
solved structures of reference-like strains), not per isolate. A B-cell score is therefore
a property of the reference antigen; per-isolate variability is carried by the conservation
layer (§10), and the two are combined only at integration (§14). Neither predictor is
benchmarked against experimentally mapped HSV B-cell epitopes in this study, so the scores
are used as a prioritisation signal and never as evidence that a region is an epitope.

`09_scripts/step28_bcell_predictors.py` is the adapter, not the predictor. The predictors
are third-party code in their own virtual environments; their raw output is archived under
`13_epitopes/bcell_raw/` so the pipeline is reproducible from the repository without
re-running a 2.6 GB protein language model. Exact versions, model checkpoints, run modes,
licences and citations are written to
`13_epitopes/bcell_predictor_provenance.tsv` by that step rather than transcribed here.

### 16.1 Linear epitopes — BepiPred-3.0

BepiPred-3.0 encodes each sequence with ESM-2 (`esm2_t33_650M_UR50D`) and passes the
per-residue embeddings to a 5-fold feed-forward ensemble trained on antibody–antigen
crystal contacts (≤4 Å). It was run over all 40 reference proteins (20 genes × 2 types,
23,184 residues) in `vt_pred` mode with the published default threshold 0.1512 and a
9-residue rolling window for the linear-epitope score. torch 2.5.1+cu121 on GPU.

The adapter's one hard check is **register**: every residue letter in the predictor output
must equal the reference protein residue at the same 1-based position, for every protein.
A single mismatch aborts the step and writes nothing, because a silent off-by-one would
place every B-cell score on the wrong residue and nothing downstream would notice. All
23,184 residues across all 40 proteins passed.

8,647 of 23,184 residues (37.3%) sit at or above the default threshold. That proportion is
high, and it is why the threshold flag is reported but the **continuous** score is what
enters the ranking: BepiPred-3.0's own documentation states that the optimal threshold is
protein-specific and that a single global cutoff is unlikely to be optimal for all
antigens. The per-gene mean linear-epitope score is highest for UL49 (VP22; 0.216 HSV-1,
0.214 HSV-2), UL1 (gL; 0.201 HSV-2, 0.189 HSV-1) and UL54 (ICP27; 0.184 both types) —
all proteins with substantial low-complexity and disordered content, which is a known
direction of bias for contact-based B-cell predictors and a reason not to read the gene
ranking as a ranking of vaccine value.

The scores reach the candidate table as `bcell_linear_mean_score`, the mean linear-epitope
score over the residues each predicted class I peptide spans (median 0.120 across the
12,771 ranked candidate rows, range 0.018–0.323). It is an annotation column: it does not
enter the primary ranking score and it does not filter anything.

### 16.2 Conformational epitopes — DiscoTope-3.0

DiscoTope-3.0 takes a 3D structure, encodes it with the ESM-IF1 inverse-folding model
(`esm_if1_gvp4_t16_142M_UR50`), adds structural features, and scores each residue with a
100-model XGBoost ensemble. It was run in `--struc_type solved`, **single-chain** mode, the
mode the model was trained in, on the same three structures used for the surface-exposure
layer (§15): 2C36 (HSV-1 gD, US6), 2GUM (HSV-1 gB, UL27) and 3M1C (HSV-2 gH–gL, UL22 and
UL1). Of the 11 chains the tool extracted, 7 were scored; the other 4 (2C36 C/D/E, 3M1C C)
carry no amino-acid backbone and are exactly the chains §15 never assigned to a gene.

Every scored residue was joined to reference numbering through
`17_structures/structure_residue_annotation.tsv`, the map §15 already owns, and the residue
letter was checked on both sides. **3,145 of 3,145 rows mapped, with no unmapped rows and
no residue-letter mismatches** — the conformational layer and the exposure layer describe
exactly the same residues.

Three properties of the output matter for how it is reported.

**The published high-confidence threshold is never reached.** Scores span 0.0005–0.659
(median 0.071; 95th percentile 0.297). No residue reaches 0.90, and only 9 of 3,145 reach
0.50. The `discotope_high_confidence` column is therefore `False` everywhere, and it is
retained only so that the threshold is visibly recorded as unmet rather than silently
dropped. As with BepiPred, the continuous score is what is used.

**Replicate chains agree, which is an internal reproducibility check rather than a
validation.** 2C36 and 2GUM each contain multiple crystallographic copies of the same
protein, scored independently. Agreement between copies at the same reference residue is
Pearson *r* = 0.956 for 2C36 gD (n = 274, median absolute difference 0.015) and *r* =
0.886–0.904 across the three 2GUM gB copies (n = 570, median absolute difference 0.011).
The residual disagreement is a floor on the precision of any single-structure score. 3M1C
contributes no replicate: its two chains are different proteins (gH and gL).

**The scores carry a real but small out-of-sample signal.** DiscoTope-3.0 has no access to
IEDB, and the `known_epitope` flag in §15 is derived independently (§11), so the comparison
is genuinely out of sample: residues inside a curated IEDB epitope score higher than the
rest (median 0.089 vs 0.061; Mann–Whitney one-sided *p* = 5.3 × 10⁻¹⁵, n = 1,294 vs 1,851).
The score also correlates with the Shrake–Rupley relative solvent accessibility computed
independently in §15 (*r* = 0.373; mean score 0.139 for exposed vs 0.074 for buried
residues), which is expected, since antibody contact requires exposure. Neither result is a
performance estimate: the effect sizes are small, and no HSV-specific benchmark was run.

Per-gene means are 0.277 (HSV-2 gL/UL1, n = 146), 0.155 (HSV-1 gD/US6, n = 548), 0.080
(HSV-1 gB/UL27, n = 1,732) and 0.056 (HSV-2 gH/UL22, n = 719). gL's high mean rests on the
smallest chain in the set and should not be read as a gene-level ranking.

Two limitations are structural rather than statistical. Single-chain mode scores each chain
in isolation, so for 2C36 (gD with nectin-1 removed) and 3M1C (gH and gL scored separately)
the partner's buried interface is treated as exposed; this is the mode the model expects,
but it means interface residues are scored as if the partner were absent. And only 4 of the
20 genes have a solved structure at all, so the conformational layer covers a fifth of the
panel and says nothing about the rest. Results for those genes are missing, not negative.

The conformational scores are written to `13_epitopes/bcell_conformational_scores.tsv` and
sit alongside the structural annotation. They do not enter the candidate ranking: the
shortlist is built on linear class I peptides, and a conformational propensity is not a
property of a peptide.

## 17. CD4 / HLA class II prediction

**NetMHCIIpan-4.3** was run on the 40 reference proteins over the 27-allele IEDB class II
reference set (Greenbaum et al.), the direct analogue of the 27-allele class I panel in
§12. The predictor is licence-gated — it is distributed only through DTU Health Tech's
academic registration form and has no public source repository — so the predictions were
produced on the DTU web server in two submissions (15 DR alleles, then 12 DQ/DP pairs,
the server's cap being 15 per submission) and adapted by
`09_scripts/step29_classii_predictions.py`. That script is an adapter and never calls the
predictor. The raw server output is archived gzipped under `13_epitopes/classii_raw/`,
because the server deletes results after 24 hours and they cannot be regenerated.

Parameters, taken from the command line the server recorded in each file rather than
assumed: 15-mers, `-BA` (binding affinity in addition to eluted-ligand likelihood),
terminal context encoding, and %Rank thresholds of 1% for strong and 5% for weak binders.
Those thresholds are NetMHCIIpan-4.3's own defaults and are looser than class I by
convention; note they differ from the 2%/10% used by NetMHCIIpan-4.0 and by IEDB, so the
adapter reads them from each file and refuses to combine files run with different settings.

Each of the 27 allele names was checked against the server's `MHC_allele_names.txt` before
submission; all 27 are present. Three spellings are in circulation for these molecules —
IEDB's (`HLA-DRB1*01:01`, `HLA-DQA1*05:01/DQB1*02:01`), the server's free-text form
(`DRB1_0101`, `HLA-DQA10501-DQB10201`) and the server's own dropdown form
(`DRB1*0101`) — and the adapter normalises all three onto IEDB nomenclature, which is what
the population coverage step joins on. One panel difference is recorded rather than
reconciled: IEDB states it has no predictor for HLA-DPA1\*02:01/DPB1\*14:01, while
NetMHCIIpan-4.3 does, so the coverage in §13.1 rests on 27 alleles where an IEDB-computed
figure would rest on 26.

**Verification.** 610,848 predictions (22,624 15-mers × 27 alleles). Every peptide was
checked to occur in its reference protein at the position the predictor reports; a single
mismatch aborts the step and writes nothing, since a silent off-by-one would place every
class II binder on the wrong residue. All 610,848 passed, as did checks that both runs
cover identical peptide sets, that their allele sets are disjoint and union to 27, and that
each protein is covered contiguously from position 1 to length − 14.

One feature of 4.3 needed explicit handling. The model scores **inverted binding**, where
the peptide lies in the groove back-to-front, and in that case the reported binding core is
the *reverse* of a subsequence of the peptide rather than a subsequence of it. This fires
on 13,362 rows (4.9% of the DQ/DP run) and exclusively on HLA-DP; it never occurs on DR or
DQ. The `Inverted` flag is carried through, and the core was verified as a forward
subsequence wherever the flag is 0 and a reversed one wherever it is 1, for all 610,848
rows. Without the flag the core column would be silently wrong for those rows.

4,861 peptide–allele pairs (0.8%) are strong binders and 25,855 (4.2%) are weak or better.
That 0.8% is close to the 1% a correctly calibrated %Rank should produce against a random
background, which is a basic sanity check on the rank column rather than a result.

**How the scores are used.** The candidate table carries
`classii_best_percentile_rank`, but it is an annotation and not a filter, for the same
reason as the B-cell scores: it is the best rank over 27 alleles and every 15-mer
overlapping the peptide, so it is a minimum over roughly a hundred values and is not
comparable to a single-allele rank. It falls below 1% for 76% of candidates. Per-allele
class II binding is in `13_epitopes/classii_binders_by_allele.tsv` and is what should be
used for anything quantitative.

No class II predictor is benchmarked in this study. There is no curated set of
experimentally validated HSV class II epitopes assembled here to benchmark against, so the
class II arm is prediction only, and no claim is made that any peptide is a CD4 epitope.

## 18. Reproducibility

Package versions are pinned in `requirements.txt` (Python 3.11). External tools: IQ-TREE
2.4.0 and the NCBI `datasets` CLI are bundled in `hsv_curated_dataset/bin/`; HyPhy
2.5.103(MP) runs in the `hyphy/hyphy` Docker image, with the exact image digest recorded in
the provenance JSON of each HyPhy stage. `19_supplements/S5_software_versions.tsv` is
generated from the running environment rather than transcribed.

A Snakemake workflow (`Snakefile` at the repository root) declares every stage and its
dependencies. Its rule graph and script references have been checked; it has not been
dry-run, because snakemake is not installed in the environment the pipeline was executed
in, and each stage is also runnable as a standalone script in the order the README gives.
Stages 1–7 are declared as frozen inputs rather than rules: re-downloading from NCBI would
change the cohort, and the analysed set is pinned by accession in
`19_supplements/S2_final_accessions.tsv`.
